# Finding real savings

Only report a deal if it is actually cheaper on a **like-for-like** basis (same baggage, cancellation terms, room type, insurance). A cheaper fare with no bag is usually not a deal.

## Flights
1. **Flex dates:** check ±3 days, and midweek vs weekend. Tue/Wed/Sat departures are often cheaper. Report the saving versus the user's ideal date so they can decide.
2. **Nearby airports:** include alternates (London: LHR/LGW/STN/LTN; NYC: JFK/EWR/LGA). Add ground transfer cost.
3. **One-way pairs:** two one-ways on different airlines can beat a return, especially with low-cost carriers.
4. **Booking window:** domestic ~1-3 months out, international ~2-6 months, peak holidays earlier. Don't promise "book now or it rises"; that is a guess.
5. **Positioning / hidden-city / throwaway tickets:** mention these exist and carry risk (airlines can penalise; skipping legs cancels the rest). Don't recommend them unprompted; if asked, explain the risks clearly.
6. **Error fares and sales:** search for current ones (Secret Flying, Going, Fly4Free, Jack's Flight Club) and say that error fares may be honoured or cancelled.
7. **Points and miles:** use loyalty programs from the profile; compare cash vs points value (cents per point) before suggesting a redemption.

## Hotels
- Compare the hotel's own site, OTAs and member rates; check if "free cancellation" lets the user rebook if prices drop.
- Shoulder season and midweek stays; city tax and resort fees can erase a "deal".
- Loyalty status, card benefits (breakfast, upgrades, 4th night free) and corporate or student rates the user qualifies for.
- Packages (flight + hotel) are sometimes cheaper; check but verify refund terms.

## Car hire
- Book early with free cancellation, then re-check closer to the date and rebook if cheaper.
- Off-airport pickup, weekly rates (7 days can be cheaper than 5), and third-party excess insurance vs supplier counter prices.
- Credit-card rental cover to avoid supplier insurance.

## Honesty rules
- Say "no meaningful saving found" when that's true.
- Treat "discount codes" from random coupon sites as unverified; mention only if a price check confirms it.
- Be wary of urgency messages ("2 rooms left!"); don't repeat them as facts.
